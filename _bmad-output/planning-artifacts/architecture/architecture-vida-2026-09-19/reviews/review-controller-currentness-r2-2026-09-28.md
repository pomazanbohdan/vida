# AD-37 / AD-40 — second-pass currentness and security-source review

Reviewed: 2026-09-28. Scope: current [AD-37/AD-40](../ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md), especially existing-Device revoke conflicts, new-Device invitations and kit rotation; cross-check against the [recovery SPEC](../../../../specs/spec-vida-persona-recovery/SPEC.md). Primary sources only. Read-only review; no source contract changed.

## Verdict

**Conditional pass; no new contradictory adopted behavior. One P2 implementation/source-boundary caution.** The revision closes the prior existing-grant ambiguity: a peer that sees an unresolved renewal/revoke conflict suspends that Device's new protected access and accepted operations, while acknowledging that already held plaintext cannot be recalled. It also distinguishes compatible grants from disputed grants and keeps dual restore provisional without a current frontier. The invitation and revoke claims still require the named `OQ-0022/0024` proof before production.

## P2 — Time-limited, single-use invitations need a partition-aware acceptance rule

AD-40 now binds QR, file and text invitations to a specific new Device key, requires explicit approval from an existing trusted Device, and calls invitations short-lived and single-use. This is an appropriate product rule. [OWASP Forgot Password](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html) recommends expiring, single-use reset tokens, but explicitly notes that lifetime restrictions do not apply to every offline method. [OWASP Transaction Authorization](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Transaction_Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html) recommends a limited authorization window and operation-unique credentials to resist replay. Both are security guidance for different workflows, not proof that independent VIDA peers can enforce one globally consumed invitation or a common expiry under partition.

The same invitation copied into file/text/QR form can reach multiple peers. Binding it to one public key blocks substitution of another Device key, but does not by itself serialize duplicate redemptions by that key or prove expiry without a trusted time/acceptance rule. A local used-token cache is insufficient across disconnected peers. AD-40 correctly leaves cross-partition single-use enforcement and freshness proof under `OQ-0022/0024`; REC-F20 should reject duplicate accepted grants after replay and demonstrate what stays pending when peers cannot establish the needed order. The invitation lifetime should be distinguished from AD-18's indefinitely retained **offline mutation candidate**: expiry denies a new grant but need not delete a local draft or audit event. This is an implementation gate, not a demand to change the approved short-lived behavior.

## Source boundaries confirmed

- [OWASP Authorization](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html) calls for permission checks on every request. [OWASP Key Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Key_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html) calls for key compromise/recovery planning and revocation checking. Those support AD-40's post-discovery suspension and AD-37's urgent old-kit revocation direction. They cannot guarantee instantaneous cutoff at an offline peer, or erase plaintext already received. “Urgent” must denote prompt revocation action plus the eventually verified effective cut, not retroactive erasure.
- [Signal Linked Devices](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007320551-Linked-Devices) documents linking through a registered primary Device, with explicit primary-side QR approval and later primary availability requirements. It is precedent for user-visible approval, but remains a contrast to VIDA's equal peers and does not establish VIDA's cross-partition invitation rule.
- [Delta Chat FAQ](https://delta.chat/en/help) documents QR transfer from an existing Device and subsequent independent operation of both Devices, with manual backup transfer as another path. It does not document VIDA's key-bound, single-use controller grants or conflicted revocation semantics. The recovery kit remains a separate flow after loss of all trusted Devices.
- The DID projection distinction and Matrix event-graph analogy from the [first currentness review](review-controller-currentness-2026-09-28.md) remain unchanged; neither supplies controller freshness or a no-winner access rule.
