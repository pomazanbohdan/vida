# Decision-critical authority check

Primary sources checked 2026-09-19. Verdicts assess `authority-r1.md`; product-design conclusions are inferences, not source mandates.

1. **Raft majority ≠ owner authorization — supported.** [Raft paper](https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf), §§2, 5: a majority elects a server leader and commits replicated-log entries under a non-Byzantine, crash/recovery failure model. Its voting rules concern server term/log state, not human-owner identity or consent. Thus a committed entry is not *by itself* proof of owner authorization. Do not infer that Raft forbids an application from adding authorization checks.

2. **`2-of-N` approval ≠ replica quorum — supported as a distinction, not a prescribed policy.** [RFC 9591](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9591.html), abstract and §1: a threshold number of key-share holders cooperate to form one signature; the RFC is Informational, not an Internet Standard. [Raft paper](https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf), §5: the majority is of configured replica servers for log agreement. These could both numerically be `2-of-3`, but their actors, evidence, and failure models differ. FROST alone does not establish that a share holder is an authorized co-owner or that the signed action was durably accepted; VIDA must define the signer-to-owner policy separately.

3. **Booking-create timeout = unknown outcome; Stripe same-key retry is bounded — supported with provider scope.** [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html), §§9.2.2, 13.1.1: a response can be lost after a successful state change, and non-idempotent requests should not be auto-retried without knowledge that repeating is safe or that the first request was unapplied. [Stripe idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests): same key and parameters replay the saved first status/body (including `500`) once endpoint execution starts; keys **may be pruned after they are at least 24 hours old**, and reuse after pruning creates a new request. Validation failures and concurrent pre-execution conflicts are not cached. Corrections: this is *Stripe's* API contract, not evidence of a Booking.com create endpoint's idempotency or a guaranteed exact 24-hour expiry. Persist operation ID/payload/key before the call; persist a provider object ID **when learned**, not before it exists. A timed-out create stays unknown until provider-specific reconciliation resolves it.

4. **Booking.com approval ≠ confirmed reservation — supported for `INQUIRY`, qualified for `RTB`.** [Request to Book overview](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/request-to-book/overview): an `INQUIRY` approval still requires guest completion; otherwise no reservation. `bookingRequestId` and `reservationId` are distinct, with the latter associated with `Booked`. The same page states the `RTB` mode has payment preauthorization and **no separate guest-confirmation step** after partner approval; pending `RTB` requests hold inventory from creation, and the connectivity partner must block/release provider-side availability. [RtB 2.0 overview](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/request-to-book/rtb-2.0/overview) separately says partner approval directly turns an `RTB` pending request into a confirmed reservation. Correction: never generalize the overview's three-step guest-finalization flow to `RTB`; distinguish `INQUIRY` from `RTB` and an inventory hold from a `BOOKED` status/reservation ID. The source does not prove that a locally sent approval request *succeeded* when its response is missing.

**Overall:** No contradiction in the core four distinctions. Material overclaim risk lies in treating Stripe's contract as portable to booking creation, treating FROST participation as verified owner consent, or treating all Booking.com RtB approvals as pre-reservation only. Source URLs above were accessed 2026-09-19.
